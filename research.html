My Research


Game-Based Assessment

Traditional mathematics assessments can unintentionally introduce barriers that prevent students from demonstrating what they know. My research explores how game-based assessment can create more equitable measures of student learning by reducing stereotype threat, increasing engagement, and improving assessment validity. Drawing on principles from educational psychology, learning sciences, and human-computer interaction, I investigate how game mechanics influence student performance and develop assessment experiences that more accurately capture students' mathematical understanding while reducing inequalities in mathematics education.

LLM-Supported Socratic Dialogue and Self-Reflection

Reflection is a critical component of deep learning, yet learners often receive little guidance in how to reflect effectively. This project investigates how large language models can facilitate Socratic dialogue that encourages learners to explain their reasoning, question their assumptions, and develop stronger metacognitive skills. By combining conversational AI with principles from the learning sciences, we aim to design AI tutors that support meaningful self-reflection rather than simply providing answers, ultimately fostering deeper understanding and more transferable learning.

LLMs for Workforce Training

Large language models have the potential to transform workforce training by providing personalized instruction, adaptive feedback, and scalable learning support. As part of Carnegie Mellon's Accenture Center of Excellence for AI (ACE-AI) , I investigate how generative AI can be effectively integrated into online workforce training environments. This work explores AI-assisted content generation, intelligent tutoring, learning analytics, and personalized learning pathways to help learners develop technical skills more efficiently while providing instructors and organizations with better insights into learning outcomes.

CLUE-AI: Critical Literacy for Uncovering Errors in AI

As AI systems become increasingly integrated into everyday life, AI literacy is becoming an essential skill. CLUE-AI is an interactive murder mystery game that teaches players to identify and critically evaluate different forms of AI bias through gameplay. Players investigate a fictional crime while working alongside an AI assistant whose evidence summaries intentionally exhibit biases such as confirmation bias, representation bias, historical bias, and automation bias. The game encourages players to question AI-generated information and develop the critical thinking skills needed to use AI responsibly.
